Blue Banner: team meeting

Monday after school, out on the back lawn: the whole of 5026 sits in a circle on the grass in team merch while the co-captains give the week's shout-outs and say what's coming up. Watch the meeting, then walk the lawn and talk to the team before everyone heads home.

Try it

How it's made

  • The lawn and the school: the same scale as the arena, 8 pixels to the foot across and 2.75 deep, so the lawn is two screens wide and the view scrolls with her. Along the top runs the school's sand-coloured block wall with its classroom windows, the robotics shop's roll-up door open on 5026's elevator robot (the intro's own sprite) and a pegboard of tools, the IRON PANTHERS sign and the team's banner. Below it, a concrete path with a bench, a thank-you card for a sponsor on an easel and dropped bags, then the grass with two trees and the sign-up table.
  • Daylight in the game's colours: the sky is the palette's own blues in dithered bands, the way the intro builds its night sky, with flat clouds drifting over the roof and a few birds. The grass, leaves, block and concrete are new three-tone ramps beside the palette, lit from the upper left with an ink outline on everything, clouds included; the grass is kept dark, the way the arena keeps its carpet, so the team's red stands out on it. The sun is hard-edged: window glints and flat tree shade, never a glow. Inside the shop it's the arena's night navies.
  • The circle: seen 20 degrees down, a circle on the grass is a flat ellipse, so the people at the back face you, the front row has its backs to you and the sides are in profile: every one of the rig's eight directions is used. Whoever is looking at something turns to it in the ground's own proportions.
  • New poses on the shared rig: everyone is the same small 3D figure as the strategist, turned and drawn to pixels. For the meeting it learned to sit cross-legged on the grass, to clap and cheer sitting down, and, standing, to clap, throw both arms up and make a point with one hand. Cheering, the arms go up in a V that clears the big hand-drawn head from every side. Every pose works for everyone in all eight directions.
  • Team merch: every student wears the red 5026 tee, the red hoodie with the panther badge, or the black alternate tee, and some the red team cap. Twenty-odd people need more faces than the five head templates, so four more are drawn the same way: a short crop, a ponytail with a red scrunchie, long hair parted in the middle and a bald mentor with a moustache.
  • The cut scene: it fades in on the sky with the place card, tilts down the wall to the team, and cuts between wide shots of the circle and close-ups at exactly twice the size. Tessa gives five shout-outs and the circle claps between them, the praised getting up to throw their arms up; the strategist answers modestly and the teammate teases her; Owen gives the updates, clipboard in hand (next week's regional, the vans at six the day before, safety glasses, permission slips due Friday with a groan, an outreach demo); then IRON PANTHERS ON THREE.
  • Who you can talk to: eleven people, each in their own voice: Tessa and Owen, the co-captains; the teammate; Bea, the build lead, looking in at the robot (the two-stage elevator, the hatch ports); Kenji, the programmer, under a tree with his laptop (the sandstorm and vision code); Luis, the scouting lead, and Mr. Brandt, the teacher, on the bench; Nadia, the outreach lead, at the sign-up table (sponsors and the Chairman's Award); Jamal, the drive coach (who's on 5026's drive team, and cycles); Eli, a nervous freshman; and Ms. Okafor, the mentor (safety glasses and gracious professionalism).

The scene is in this folder: layout.js, place.js, people.js, cutscene.js, lines.js and scene.js. It runs on the shared stage.js (the controller, the camera, talking and the cut scene clock) and talk.js, and draws everyone with walker.js and cast.js, the robot in the shop with sprites.js and the colours and letters with pixel.js.
