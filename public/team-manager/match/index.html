Blue Banner: match lab

A real 2019 match, simulated and played out on the arena's field. Six robots, each with its own abilities and its alliance's plan for it, drive the field for two and a half minutes, and the score is only ever what you see go in: each hatch and each ball lands in its port at the moment the scoreboard ticks. Watch a robot miss, a defender shut a scorer down, an intake fumble, a climb go wrong. Change the plan below and play the same match again (the same seed is the same luck).

    The robots

    Every kind of robot the simulation knows, blue and red, in the states a match shows: resting, holding a hatch, holding cargo, reaching the cargo ship's lip, a rocket's level 2 and level 3 (each kind as high as it goes), up on its stilts or arm mid-climb, and dead after a breakdown.

    How it works

    The match
    sim.js plays the 2019 game, Deep Space, by the 2019 manual, a tenth of a second at a time. Before the match: empty rockets, a cargo in each of the cargo ship's front bays, a null hatch panel (grey) or a cargo in each side bay, 6 cargo in each depot, a hatch panel waiting at each loading station, and each robot holding a hatch panel or a cargo. The sandstorm (15 s): black shutters hang over the driver stations' windows, so robots drive off their HAB (3 points from level 1, 6 from level 2) and score by camera, a little slower and less accurate; at 0:15 the shutters roll up, the ship's bays tilt, and any cargo not behind a hatch panel rolls out. Teleop (135 s): cycles, hatch panels (2) from the loading stations and cargo (3) from the stations or the depots, onto two rockets and the cargo ship's eight bays. Cargo only stays behind a hatch panel: in the rocket it goes in by the level's port and rolls to one of the two bays (either, or the empty one), and rolls back out if that bay has no hatch panel. The endgame: home to the HAB for level 1 (3), 2 (6) or 3 (12); a missed climb drops back to level 1. Ranking points: 2 for a win, 1 for a complete rocket, 1 for 15 or more climb points.
    Defense
    The Showdown's rule: a defended robot keeps 1 minus (defender's ability times its susceptibility) of what it does, while they're touching: it moves and works slower and misses more. A harder pusher can pin its target for up to five seconds, then has to back off. As in the manual: no defense in the sandstorm, one defender per alliance at a time, and a robot inside its own HAB zone can't be touched. Robots with nothing left to score go and defend.
    What's simplified
    No fouls. Robots only touch when one is defending the other. Nobody picks a dropped piece back up off the floor. The near wall's rockets are behind the camera, so they're drawn see-through, their pieces solid.
    The robots
    robots.js has nine kinds and the opening's teams, each tuned so its simulated average lands near the one in the story (7461 fading after Friday, 6834's rebuilt intake), and bots.js draws them. animate.js turns the timeline into poses at any moment, and draw.js puts them on the field.

    In this folder: sim.js (the match), robots.js (the robots and teams), animate.js (the match at any moment), bots.js (the sprites), draw.js (the picture) and lab.js (this page). From the address bar: ?seed=N, ?t=SECONDS, ?preset=qf. Keys: Space plays and pauses, the arrows step.
