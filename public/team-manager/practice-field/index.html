Blue Banner: practice field

Back at school, in the cafeteria after the last bell. The lunch tables are folded against the wall, and team 5026 has taped grey carpet over the tiles and built half a 2019 field on it from plywood and 2x4s. The late sun streams in through the tall windows while the robot runs timed hatch cycles and the mentor calls out the times. The cut scene follows the light down onto the field for one timed cycle. Then it's yours to walk: round the field, never across it, because the carpet is off limits while the robot is running. Talk to the drive team, the scouts, the programmer, the battery captain and the custodian, who wants his tables back. She comes in by the hallway doors at the left-hand end of the room (skip the cut scene and she walks in from the left), and the way out is the side doors at the right-hand end, out to the parking lot.

How it's made

  • The room: the cafeteria is seen the way the arena is: its long back wall faces you and the floor recedes from it, at 8 pixels to the foot along and 2.75 to the foot of depth. The wall is painted block over a teal tile wainscot, kept dim like the arena's stands so the people and the field stand out against it. Along it: the hallway doors under an EXIT sign, six tall windows with posters and a clock reading 4:40 between them, the team's red 5026 banner over two folded lunch tables, the serving line with its shutter down, and two vending machines. Out on the floor are the programmer's table with a laptop, the scouting table with pizza boxes, and the custodian's cart.
  • The way out: the right-hand end of the room is the outside wall, drawn the way the World Championship hall's end walls are: its face above the floor at the back, then its thickness cut away a few inches up, running down the edge of the room. Out in the front half it breaks for the side doors to the parking lot, standing open (they swing outward, so only their aluminium frame and threshold show), with the walk outside bright in the sun, the same straw as the sun's patches on the tiles. The daylight through them lies across the tiles and the rubber mat inside in two hard steps, sheared like the windows' patches, so the way out shows from across the room; a fire alarm is on the wall nearby, and a red EXIT sign, like the one over the hallway doors she came in by, hangs over the opening at the height of the doors' top, on a post up out of the door frame. Walk into the doorway (or push right anywhere along that wall) and she heads out; on this page she comes back in at her mark, and in the game the van ride starts.
  • The sunlight: the sun is low behind the wall, in the first window. Through the glass the sky comes in flat bands with a checkerboard dither between them, over the trees and the school's other wing in silhouette. Each window throws its own six panes onto the floor, sheared to the right and squashed, hard-edged, with the frame bars as shadow lines and a bright rim on the side toward the sun. A patch is a fixed pale straw, the same hue on the carpet and the tiles and clearly paler and yellower than the plywood standing in it, so it reads as light, still showing the carpet's speckle and its tape. Each window's shaft runs from under the sill down to its patch in two flat steps, brighter nearer the window, with a checkerboard between and a bright rim down its lit edge: the wainscot a lighter teal under it, the tiles a warm light grey. It's painted on the wall and the floor so it never tints what stands in it, every ink outline stays ink, and dust drifts down through it.
  • The homemade field: the blue half of the arena's 2019 field, the same size and in the same places, built the way teams build practice fields: three rolls of carpet with duct tape over the seams, white tape lines laid by hand, a plywood rocket with velcro hatch ports, half a plywood cargo ship, a HAB of plywood and 2x4s, a loading station, and a driver station of 2x4 posts with plywood across the bottom three feet, 5026 painted on it, and clear polycarbonate above so the drivers can see the field, drawn clear like the arena's field walls so you see the drive team through it. The hatch panels and cargo are the real yellow and orange ones. Beside it is the team's gear: a battery cart with its chargers blinking, a hatch rack, a basket of cargo, a red tool chest, and a rolling whiteboard with the week's cycle times.
  • The robot's timed cycles: 5026's elevator robot from the intro, on blue practice bumpers. It takes a hatch through the loading station's slot, spins round, runs along the far wall to the rocket, lifts and places the hatch on level 2, then lowers, spins and runs back, and the next cycle goes to level 3, which takes longer, because the elevator has further to go: 9.2 seconds on level 2, 10.4 on level 3, the times the mentor gives. Its runs are eased, its wheels turn with the distance it covers, and its signal light blinks while it's enabled and holds solid while it's disabled. Between reps the team resets the field the safe way: as the robot comes to rest at the station the operator disables it, and only then does a student step on from the carpet's edge, pull the hatch back off the rocket and run back off; then the robot is enabled again for the next rep.
  • The people: everyone is the same rig as the strategist, in their own clothes with their own hand-drawn head: the teammate as drive coach, a driver and an operator on controllers and the human player holding the next hatch, all behind the station wall; the mentor in a teal polo with her stopwatch and the scouting lead with her tablet, and another scout with a tablet at the far table; the programmer at his laptop; the battery captain at the cart; the safety captain just in front of the carpet's tape; and the custodian by his cart. Ten of them have something to say, about timing cycles, splitting the drive team's work, lining up on the rocket's angled faces, battery care, scouting your own practice, staying off the carpet, disabling the robot for the reset and calling out before it's enabled, and the e-stop. While you talk to someone at the front of the room, the camera eases down so both faces stay clear of the dialog box.
  • The cut scene: it fades in on the sun in the window and slides down the light onto the field. The drive team gets ready, the operator calls "enabling!" and enables the robot, and the mentor counts it down; the robot runs one timed hatch cycle, the camera cuts back to the station as it comes home, and the mentor calls out the time. The drive team cheers with their arms up in a V, the custodian has a word about the tables, and the strategist runs in through the hallway doors as the mentor calls the time over to her, then runs round the front of the field to her mark. She answers from there, where you take over.

The field's pieces follow field.js, the robot and the game pieces are sprites.js's, and the people are walker.js with cast.js looks, all shared with the arena; this scene's own modules are in the practice-field folder. The controller, the camera and the talking are stage.js, shared by every scene.
