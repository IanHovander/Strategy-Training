Blue Banner: the opening

The first minute of the game. Saturday, 12:38 PM, alliance selection. Her team is seed 4, an alliance captain, and the senior strategist who was meant to make the pick has just texted in sick. Down on the floor with the team reps, the co-captain hands her the pick list, and Norbert for luck. Then MC Robin calls her up to the field, and the 45-second pick clock starts. The clock counts down over the scouting tablet: two teams to choose from, and one thing to notice. Then the second pick: the goal on her whiteboard is to beat alliance 5 in the quarterfinal, best 2 of 3, and while she studies the board the other alliances pick, until it's her turn again.

The shots

  1. Black, 0.7 s: the crowd's noise rising, drawn as a line of sound across the dark, with phone screens and camera flashes up in the stands.
  2. Her phone, 2.4 s: full screen, in her hand. Her "WHERE ARE YOU??" is still unanswered, then the typing dots, then "SICK. CAN'T COME." and "YOU'VE GOT THIS.", the phone buzzing as each arrives. The clock on the phone says 12:37. Once both texts are in, it waits for A, so there's time to read them.
  3. The card, about 3 s: ALLIANCE SELECTION, SATURDAY 12:38 PM, the minute ticking over from 12:37, with MC Robin over the PA: "WELCOME TO ALLIANCE SELECTION!"
  4. The arena, 1.2 s: wide from the floor in front of the field, the house lights down. Sign 4's spotlight is lit full with nobody in it; the other captains and Robin by the mic are in half light. Straight below the empty sign, apart from the other team reps, Riley and the strategist stand in the dark with their backs to you, until a spotlight snaps on over them. The brightest things in the shot are her empty place and the two of them.
  5. The co-captain, 1.4 s, silent: close on the two of them, every other light out and the arena gone nearly black: just the two of them in their light. Riley turns from the field to her, Norbert hugged to their chest, and she turns to Riley. This shot holds, so A can't skip it.
  6. The handoff: "THE LIST IS YOURS." Riley holds Norbert out: "AND TAKE HIM. HE'S BEEN TO EVERY EVENT SINCE 2014." Then a cut to their hands at twice the size as Norbert passes from Riley's red sleeves to her, and his 26 ear glints once. This shot holds too.
  7. Her: close on her with Norbert in her arms: "...OKAY." From here on, Norbert sits in the top left corner with his battery voltage.
  8. Her call, about 2 s: the first three alliances have picked meanwhile. Robin calls "5026, COME ON UP!" over the audience display, which shows alliances 1 to 3 filled, row 4 lit and the clock starting from 0:45: the pick clock runs from the moment she's called. Then a cut to her walking up through the gate to the field, with Riley watching her go. From here the words turn by themselves.
  9. The pick: a hard cut to the scouting tablet. The clock slams in, still running from the call, a few seconds gone. The stick (or the arrow keys) moves between the two cards, A picks, and the invite asks INVITE or BACK. Then the bet log: a right pick goes on to the second pick; a wrong one shows why and rewinds the clock, Norbert's ear glowing.
  10. The goal: after the right first pick, the dashboard: the alliances so far, 6834 just in. Her goal card slides in, a whiteboard checklist (first pick ticked; second pick, to beat alliance 5; win the quarterfinal, best 2 of 3), while Riley says what it means: the quarterfinal is alliance 4 against alliance 5, and alliance 5 has 7777, the top scorer at the event. Then the card folds up into a pill in the header, where it stays; a tap opens it again.
  11. The other picks, about 33 s: alliance selection goes on. Round 1 finishes (alliances 5 to 8) and round 2 comes back up the line (8 to 5), a pick every four seconds: Robin's call on the PA line, the pick going into its row on the alliance board, its tile on her list stamped TAKEN BY ALLIANCE n and dropping away. Alliance 5 takes a second cargo robot, then a hatch robot. She can browse her list the whole time, but can't invite anyone yet.
  12. The second pick: Robin calls her, the 45-second clock slams in, and three teams are left: 2202 (27, cargo, the biggest average left), 5518 (20, a Level 3 climb that works two times in seven) and 4471 (14, a defender whose card says it held 7777 to 22 in Q23). Then the bet log for PICK 2: the matchup as two bars. 4471 is the right pick (92 to 78, with 7777 held to 22); 2202 is a coin flip (105 to 102). The lesson: pick for the matchup, not the list. A wrong pick rewinds to her turn, not to the start; the right one ticks the goal card's second line: PICKS DONE, and A goes on to the quarterfinal.
  13. The quarterfinal, best 2 of 3: the series card, then each match quickly on the arena's field, the score climbing on the scoreboard. Match 1: 4471 stays on 7777 all match, and red wins 88 to 74. Match 2: 6834's battery browns out, and blue wins 80 to 61. 1-1.
  14. The endgame call: match 3 freezes at 0:20, red up 71 to 64. Riley on the headset, 6834's driver begging for Level 3, then the call: LEVEL 2 (+6, made 10 of 10) or LEVEL 3 (+12, made 5 of 10), with blue's 6417 going for Level 3 (made 6 of 10) and the crowd chanting LEVEL THREE. Then the climbs close up, on the intro's HAB shot, with honest dice; the buzzer; and the bet log: Level 2 wins 10 times in 10, Level 3 7 in 10. When the sure thing is enough, take it. A Level 3 call rewinds to the call, even when it works; Level 2 ticks the last goal, and it's TO BE CONTINUED: THE SEMIFINAL.

How it's made

  • The shot list is the design's (docs/blue-banner-design.md, section 5, "Building the opening"), cut like the intro: hard cuts between shots, an eased pan in the wide shot, close-ups at exactly twice the size, film bars throughout, and every frame drawn from the cut scene's clock. Until Robin calls her up, every line waits for A, so it goes at the player's pace; from the call on, the words turn by themselves and the clock is running, ticking softly every second and louder for the last ten.
  • The arena is alliance selection's, borrowed as it is: its set, its reps, MC Robin on her skates, its layout and its lights. Seeds 1 to 3 and 5 to 8 stand at their signs along the arc, each in a spotlight. Everyone else waits on the floor in front of the field, in knots, a little further left than in alliance selection to leave the floor in front of the gate clear. Riley has taken the strategist aside there, right below the gate and their empty sign, so nobody stands near them and her way up is straight through the gate. Once the first three alliances have picked, their first picks stand behind their captains.
  • Riley is a new character on the walker rig. A senior in the team's red hoodie with the panther badge, black jeans, and a hand-drawn head: a tall curly top over short sides, held back by a white sweatband, so Riley stands out from her, the teammate and every rep. Riley hugs Norbert in the walker's clap pose and holds him out in the drive pose.
  • Norbert is drawn three ways. In someone's arms he's a new 12x14 sprite, drawn by hand from the 16x19 corner one. In the handoff he's the full 36x44 sprite at twice the size. After the handoff he's in the corner, with 12.4V beside him: enough charge to rewind a few minutes, not yet enough to reach Thursday (12.9V comes after the semifinal).
  • Her phone is a dark-mode texting screen, with her fingers round its edge and her thumb on the side. The words use the game's 5x7 letters.
  • The pick is the scouting tablet (pick.js, tablet.js, teams.js): the clock and its draining bar, her robot pinned for reference, and two cards, each with the team number, a big average, one note and its ten matches as boxes. 7461 shows 38, the biggest number on the screen, but it's fading: 44 a match for its first six, 29 for its last four (Friday's last and Saturday morning's three), two thirds of it. 6834 shows 35, but it's rising: 27 for its first six, a rebuilt intake, then 48 a match for its last four, about 10% better than 7461 at its best. After the pick, the last four boxes light up, red and green. The lesson, in one line: look at the trend, not the average.
  • The second pick is the same tablet's dashboard (second.js, board.js, goals.js, verdict.js, teams2.js): a header with the clock or who's picking and the goal pill, Robin's PA line, the eight alliances filling in (hers in red, alliance 5 tagged VS US), alliance 5 as the app knows it (what each robot scores with and its average, 7777's marked BEST), her list as tiles, and the card for the one the cursor's on: number, role, average, a note, its ten matches, and a line of data with the match it's about outlined. The picks can't be hurried: they're the event's, and they're the clue. In 2019 the third robot on an alliance often played defense, so the right answer is the real game's strategy: a robot that takes 24 points off their star is worth more than one that adds 27 to a pile.

The scene is in this folder: cutscene.js (the shots), cocaptain.js, phone.js, handoff.js, norbert-held.js, pick.js, tablet.js, teams.js, the second pick's second.js, board.js, goals.js, verdict.js and teams2.js, the quarterfinal's quarter.js, match.js and series.js, and scene.js. Add ?pick2 to the address to start at the second pick, or ?pick2=turn to start at her turn; ?qf to start at the quarterfinal, or ?qf=call at its frozen moment. It borrows alliance selection's set.js, people.js, mc.js and layout.js, and runs on the shared stage.js.
